No simulations were run and no files or model settings were changed. The
  audit shows that Case 900 currently matches the benchmark’s aggregate
  capacity numerically, but not its mass placement or dynamic construction
  definition.

  ## Main finding

  The Case 900 implementation is a capacity-constrained proxy:

  [
  C_{900}=307{,}750\ \frac{\mathrm J}{\mathrm{m^2K}}
  \times 48\ \mathrm{m^2}
  =14.772\ \mathrm{{MJ/K}}.
  ]

  That total is imposed by multiplying every Case 600 layer specific heat by
  the same factor:

  [
  s_C=
  \frac{14.772}{2.73253752}
  =5.405964196971026.
  ]

  This preserves all Case 600 layer thicknesses, conductivities, densities,
  and prescribed component U-values. It does not introduce benchmark-
  prescribed heavyweight layers. Under the no-IW solver, all resulting wall,
  roof, and floor mass is placed in the exterior AW storage branch; no IW/
  internal-mass node exists.

  ## Steady-state versus dynamic quantities

  Steady-state transmission is controlled by prescribed whole-component U-
  values:

  [
  UA=\sum_i U_iA_i,
  \qquad
  R_{\mathrm{AW,total}}=\frac{1}{UA}.
  ]

  For both cases:

  [
  \begin{aligned}
  UA={}&0.53(63.6)+0.33(48)+0.038(48)+3.1(12)\
  ={}&88.572\ \mathrm{W/K},
  \end{aligned}
  ]

  and therefore:

  [
  R_{\mathrm{AW,total}}
  =\frac{1}{88.572}
  =0.01129025\ \mathrm{K/W}.
  ]

  The dynamic VDI reduction does not derive this transmission resistance from
  those U-values. It uses the explicit layer properties:

  [
  R'_j=\frac{d_j}{\lambda_j},
  \qquad
  C'j=\rho_jc{p,j}d_j.
  ]

  Layer transfer matrices are evaluated at two- and seven-day trial periods.
  The selected component reductions are subsequently combined at a five-day
  room aggregation period. Thus:

  - steady state is U-value-controlled;
  - dynamic response is layer-controlled;
  - the two descriptions are not required by the implementation to represent
    one physically consistent construction.

  ## Where the heavyweight capacity goes

  Under the no-IW solver:

  - wall capacity enters the AW component reductions;
  - roof capacity enters the AW component reductions;
  - floor capacity also enters the AW component reductions;
  - all six opaque surfaces are aggregated into one AW storage branch;
  - windows alter the parent wall’s effective (R_1) in parallel but add no
    capacitance;

  - there is no IW component, IW aggregate, IW coefficient set, or IW storage
    state.

  Therefore, the answer is unambiguous: the Case 900 heavyweight capacity is
  received exclusively by the AW storage node.

  ## Does no-IW preserve the intended mass placement?

  No.

  The benchmark/comparator representation is an abstract effective internal
  mass:

  [
  C_m=C'_mA_f,
  \qquad
  A_m=f_mA_f.
  ]

  For Case 900, that means:

  [
  C_m=14.772\ \mathrm{MJ/K},
  \qquad
  A_m=116.64\ \mathrm{m^2}.
  ]

  The present VDI implementation instead assigns the capacity to exterior
  wall, roof, and floor constructions and reduces them into an exterior-
  envelope AW branch whose room-side area is 171.6 m².

  This changes at least four physical attributes:

  1. Mass location: effective internal mass becomes exterior-envelope mass.
  2. Coupling: mass is coupled through AW resistances to the exterior
     boundary.

  3. Receiving area: 116.64 m² effective mass area is replaced by 171.6 m² AW
     area.

  4. Source interaction: projected radiant gains interact with AW storage
     rather than a distinct internal-mass node.

  Matching only

  [
  \sum C_{\rm layers}=14.772\ \mathrm{MJ/K}
  ]

  does not preserve the intended dynamic impedance, surface coupling,
  effective area, or source allocation.

  ## Layer-convention issues

  The audit identifies these unresolved convention problems:

  - Case 900 has no checked-in authoritative heavyweight layer construction.
    Every Case 900 material (c_p) is a scaled proxy.

  - The scaling creates implausible material properties, such as plasterboard
    and fiberglass specific heats around 4.54 kJ/(kg·K) and timber around
    6.49 kJ/(kg·K).

  - The Case 900 U-values remain those of Case 600, while dynamic layer
    resistance also remains unchanged. Only capacity is scaled.

  - Prescribed whole-component U-values control steady-state transmission,
    whereas explicit layers control dynamics. These are parallel conventions,
    not one reconciled construction.

  - Surface films are included in prescribed U-values but excluded or
    separately reconstructed in parts of the dynamic reduction.

  - The floor’s 1.003 m insulation layer is itself a resistance proxy, not a
    literal benchmark construction.

  - Case 600 explicit layer capacity already differs from the checked-in
    aggregate benchmark capacity; scaling those layers propagates that
    unresolved mapping into Case 900.

  - The benchmark effective mass-area factor is recorded but unused.
  - The VDI reduction converts 14.772 MJ/K of raw layers into 10.457 MJ/K of
    effective AW-node capacitance; raw capacity matching is therefore not
    equivalent to reduced-capacitance matching.

  - Treating the floor as an exterior AW storage surface further differs from
    an abstract internal effective-mass interpretation.

  The central diagnosis is therefore: Case 900’s discrepancy cannot presently
  be interpreted as a clean heavyweight-model test. The implementation uses
  the correct aggregate capacity target, but obtains it through non-benchmark
  layer proxies and places the resulting mass on the wrong VDI topology
  relative to the benchmark/comparator.
